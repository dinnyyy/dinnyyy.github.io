HarvestAI: Strawberry-Picking Robot

WIP

A Monash DeepNeuron team project: a desktop-scale robotic arm with a camera that finds ripe strawberries and picks them on its own. Strawberries are a hard target for automation, every berry ripens at its own pace, bruises easily, and half of them sit behind a leaf, which is why the job is still done by hand. The MVP is three AI components working together: a computer-vision module that detects and classifies each berry’s ripeness from a live camera feed, a decision layer that turns a detection into a 3D grasp point, and a motion-planning module that moves the arm through it, with model training accelerated on HPC clusters. Target bar for the MVP is around 80% detection accuracy, 3mm arm positioning accuracy, and a full pick-and-place cycle in about 10 seconds. I’m on the software team building the perception module: curating and augmenting the strawberry image set, then training the object-detection model everything downstream depends on.

  • Computer Vision

  • PyTorch
  • OpenCV

JackTen: NLHE Poker Bot

WIP

A no-limit hold’em strategy engine, built from the math up rather than from a library. The roadmap runs hand evaluation and equity first, then the EV, pot odds, and minimum-defense-frequency formulas that drive every decision, then Bayesian range updates and opponent stats (VPIP, PFR, 3-bet%) for exploitative play. I’m currently through the equity and EV layers. Next up is deep learning for opponent behaviour: sequence models (LSTM and, if the data supports it, transformer-based) trained on hand-history action sequences to predict opponent tendencies and ranges, feeding into the same Bayesian updates rather than replacing them.

OLS vs MLE: A Monte Carlo Study

WIP

OLS is the default regression tool, but it is only the best one when a specific set of textbook assumptions holds, and real data breaks them constantly: fat tails, non-constant variance, outcomes that are counts or yes/no rather than continuous. This project stops asserting how much that costs you and measures it. I simulate thousands of datasets where I know the true parameter, fit both OLS and the correctly specified maximum likelihood alternative to each, and track bias, efficiency, and whether a 95% confidence interval actually contains the truth 95% of the time. Everything also gets held against the Cramér-Rao lower bound, the hard limit on how precise any unbiased estimator can be: the simple Poisson rate hits that bound exactly at any sample size, the regression MLE only closes on it as the sample grows, and OLS provably sits above it once its assumptions break. Both derivations are worked by hand, with the Poisson regression MLE solved by Newton-Raphson rather than handed to a library. It ships as a written report, not just a repo.
